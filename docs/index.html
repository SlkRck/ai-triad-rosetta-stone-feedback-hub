Welcome

The AI Rosetta Stone is epistemic infrastructure for multi-perspective AI-policy analysis. It is not a chatbot and not a search engine. It maps how three schools of thought — accelerationist, safetyist, skeptic — argue about the same question, and shows you where they truly disagree and what would change minds.

Read this first: the tool's output is structural, not veridical. It shows where disagreement lives and what would resolve it — not what is true. A claim can win the argument and still be false.

Who this is for

  • Policy analysts, researchers, and strategists working through contested, normative AI questions.
  • People who want to map an argument and find blind spots — not look up facts.

How to use it well

Take the guided tour first. Inside the tool, click the Help button (the ? icon) and choose Launch Welcome Tour for a walkthrough of the main features — browsing the taxonomy, running debates, chatting with perspectives, and setting up an API key.

1. Getting in

StepWhat to do
1Open the tool. You'll land on a sign-in page.
2Choose "Browse without an account" for read-only access — enough to explore the full taxonomy and debate archive.
3Sign in (GitHub / Google / Microsoft) only to run a debate or propose an edit.

2. Come with a tension, not a fact question

It shines on contested normative questions ("Should frontier AI development slow down?"). Vague or purely factual prompts produce activity without insight.

3. Read the taxonomy first, debates second

The three-camp taxonomy is the map. See how each camp frames the same thing — "AI governance" is an innovation bottleneck (acc.), essential gating (saf.), or a capture risk (skp.). That contrast is often the whole insight.

4. Treat cruxes as the payoff

The most valuable artifact isn't the transcript — it's the crux, the point whose resolution would move the most minds. Use it as a "consult the real world here" pointer.

5. Never read "won the argument" as "true"

Survival in the graph ≠ truth. Use the tool to structure thinking and surface blind spots, not to settle debates.

6. Mind the register

When a disagreement is definitional (e.g. "accountability (market)" vs "(institutional)" vs "(algorithmic)"), that's a feature. Lean into disambiguating.

What to expect

  • Human-in-the-loop editing: edit nodes, open a PR, bump the taxonomy version. Contributions go through review, not direct writes.
  • Heavy, rich responses: debates and taxonomies can be several megabytes. Not quick chat.
  • Empty sections in anonymous mode don't mean it's broken.
