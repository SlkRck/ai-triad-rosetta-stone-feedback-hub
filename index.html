1. What this tool is (and is not)

The AI Rosetta Stone is epistemic infrastructure for multi-perspective AI-policy analysis. It is not a chatbot and not a search engine.

It does three things:

  1. Holds three fixed worldviews — accelerationist, safetyist, skeptic — as structured BDI taxonomies (every node tagged Belief, Desire, or Intention).
  2. Runs adversarial debates between those perspectives under formal rules, with a procedural moderator.
  3. Distills results into cruxes — the points whose resolution would move the most minds.
The core promise: its output is structural, not veridical. It shows where disagreement lives and what would resolve it — not what is true. A claim can dominate the argument graph and still be false. Keep this in mind; it changes how you read everything the tool produces.

2. Who it's for

  • Policy analysts, researchers, and strategists working through contested, normative AI questions.
  • People who want to map an argument and find its blind spots — not look up facts.

If your question is "what is X?", this is the wrong instrument. If it's "should we do X, and what would change my mind?", it's the right one.

4. How to use it well

Come with a genuine policy tension, not a fact question

It shines on contested normative questions. Vague or factual prompts produce activity without insight.

Read the taxonomy first, debates second

The three-camp taxonomy is the map. See how each camp frames the same situation — e.g. "AI governance" as an innovation bottleneck (acc.), essential gating (saf.), or a capture risk (skp.). That contrast is often the whole insight.

Treat cruxes as the payoff

The most valuable artifact isn't the transcript — it's the crux. Use it as a "consult the real world here" pointer.

Never read "won the argument" as "true"

Survival in the graph ≠ truth. Use the tool to structure thinking and surface blind spots, not to settle debates.

Mind the register

When a disagreement is definitional (e.g. "accountability (market)" vs "(institutional)" vs "(algorithmic)"), that's a feature. Lean into disambiguating.

5. How to interact with it

  • Phrase inputs as normative/strategic questions so the BDI decomposition has something to work with.
  • Expect a human-in-the-loop editing model: edit nodes, open a PR, bump the taxonomy version. Contributions go through review queues, not direct writes.
  • Budget for heavy, slow, rich responses. Debate records and taxonomies can be multiple megabytes. This isn't quick chat.
  • Explore edge states patiently. Empty sections in anonymous mode don't mean it's broken.

7. Troubleshooting

  • "I only see the sign-in page." The app renders client-side; use a real browser. For read-only, click "Browse without an account."
  • A section looks empty. Some areas are sparse in anonymous mode or not yet populated. Try signing in or the community library.
  • A confusing error appears. Note the exact step and message — that's useful feedback.
